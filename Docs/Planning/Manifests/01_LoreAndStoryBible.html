01

The Premise

Four months ago, Grayback County went dark. What the news called a "contamination incident" at an agricultural research annex outside Millbrook Falls became, within seventy-two hours, a hard quarantine line sixty miles around — and everyone inside who could still move was reclassified as a biohazard.

You are not a soldier. You are a contractor: one of hundreds hired by Warden Group under a no-bid federal recovery contract to go back in, again and again, for whatever the county still has worth taking — samples, salvage, sometimes bodies, whenever the pay justifies it. Nobody expects you to survive your contract. Nobody's replaced yet, either.

You work out of Grayback Lodge, a converted hunting compound the locals still half-own and Warden Group half-runs, and a handful of smaller holdouts scattered through the county that haven't been picked clean or burned out. What you do with your time in the zone is yours to decide — survive, loot, hunt contracts, or start pulling on the thread that says none of this was an accident.

03

The Outbreak

Halvorsen AgriGen ran a research annex on the north edge of Millbrook Falls developing Compound Fallow — publicly, a controlled-dormancy agent meant to humanely suppress livestock and pest populations during famine-relief operations, pitched to regulators as a genuinely humanitarian technology. What it actually did, in a live human population, was different: Fallow doesn't just induce dormancy. In a high enough dose, following either a bite or prolonged exposure, it re-animates the nervous system after clinical death and drives it toward one behavior — spreading the exposure further.

The contamination event itself is never fully explained on-screen — that ambiguity is deliberate (see §10). What's public record: an "atmospheric containment failure" at the Annex, a blackout of the surrounding six blocks within the hour, and a spreading pattern too clean and too fast for an accidental leak, though nobody outside a very short list has ever been in a position to say so out loud.

A federal quarantine order stood up the Grayback Perimeter in under four days. Millbrook Falls, Cedar Hollow, the Anders River farms, and the Grayback Highlands were all inside the line before evacuation finished. What's inside now stays inside.

04

Warden Group

A full military occupation of a county-sized biohazard zone is expensive, politically ugly, and hard to sustain past the first news cycle. So the perimeter itself is federal and permanent, but everything inside it — salvage, sample recovery, population control, contract enforcement — is subcontracted to Warden Group, a private crisis-response firm operating under a no-bid recovery contract nobody in the county seat ever got to vote on.

Warden Group doesn't send its own people past the wire more than it has to. It hires yours instead — mercenaries, salvagers, the desperate and the specialized alike — pays them in scrip redeemable at Grayback Lodge, and treats every contractor as fully expendable. A dead contractor is a line item, not an incident. That's the entire reason permadeath is the premise, not a difficulty setting: nobody sent you in expecting you back.

Publicly, Warden Group's contracts are framed as recovery and cleanup: retrieve viable tissue samples, thin the population in overrun sectors, recover Halvorsen equipment "for containment analysis." Whether that framing survives contact with what your contracts actually add up to is §8's territory.

06

Factions

Hostile — infected

The Fallow-touched

Not one enemy type — a progression. Fresh cases (recently turned, days old) are fast, aggressive, still coordinated enough to be dangerous in groups. Wasted cases (weeks to months post-turn) are slower and dumber but absorb more damage and travel in the migrating hordes the spawn-director routes through the map. Both are the same UZSZombieConfig archetype system already built — this is content variety, not new engineering.

Employer

Warden Group

Checkpoint guards, the Lodge liaison, and the contract board. Not combatants you fight under normal play — they're the reason you're here. Their sincerity is the thing §8 slowly undermines.

Hostile — human

The Fallow Company

Rogue ex-Warden Group contractors led by Marcus Voss, who figured out what Warden Group is really recovering before anyone else did — and now hold territory, guard caches, and occasionally hire out to whoever pays, zombies and mercs alike treated as equally disposable obstacles. This is BF_HumanHostileFaction's content identity.

Background — noncombatant

The Hollow Folk

Scattered civilian holdouts who never made evacuation and never joined a faction — a few at St. Anne's, more implied than shown elsewhere. Pure environmental storytelling and light dynamic-event fuel (radio chatter, found notes); not a combat faction and not scoped for dialogue trees.

07

Key Figures

Handler Okafor

Warden Group liaison, the Lodge

Gives the official contract line: sample recovery, kill quotas, equipment retrieval. Professionally warm, genuinely believes the cover story for longer than is comfortable once players start finding evidence otherwise.

"Cutter" Sam Ostrander

Lodge proprietor

Owned the hunting outfit before the quarantine, never left. Runs general trade independent of Warden Group — the freeform economy's anchor. Lost a nephew who worked at the Annex; trusts Warden Group exactly as far as he has to.

Reyes

"The Fence," County Line Truck Stop

Black-market trade, stolen Warden Group gear, and the first NPC to casually mention a rumor that doesn't match the official story — dropped as flavor, not a quest marker.

Dr. Priya Anand

Ex-Halvorsen bio-safety officer, St. Anne's Parish

Defected from the Annex before the breach went public. Gives the documentation/cure-arc contract line — the one vendor thread that pulls all the way through to §8's endgame branch.

Marcus Voss

Fallow Company commander

Former Halvorsen site-security chief, now leads the rogue PMC holding Checkpoint Delta and points beyond. Has — or claims to have — Halvorsen's own cure research. Never a dialogue NPC; encountered only through his faction and what's recovered from them.

08

The Mystery

Not advertised at game start. Delivered entirely through optional, found material — loot documents, vendor barks, environmental staging — the way Tarkov's TerraGroup evidence and STALKER 2's PDAs do it (see Manifest 06). A player who never picks up a single document still gets the full survival game with zero narrative debt.

Tier 1 — Public Briefingknown from the first session

What everyone's told

An accidental containment failure at a Halvorsen agricultural research annex caused the outbreak. Warden Group is here to help recover the county and study a cure. Your contracts serve that recovery.

Tier 2 — Field Intelligencediscoverable mid-game, optional loot & vendor dialogue

What the documents say

Internal Halvorsen memos (found in Annex-adjacent loot, sold piecemeal by Reyes) describe Fallow as a dual-use program — the "humane pest control" pitch sat alongside a parallel, unlabeled military application file. A shipping log shows a field test scheduled in the two weeks before the "accidental" breach. None of this is handed to the player outright; it's assembled from partial documents the way STALKER 2's PDA notes are.

Tier 3 — Classifiedendgame thread, Dr. Anand's contract line + Fallow Company recovery

What's actually true

The breach wasn't an accident — it was an unauthorized live-population field test, and Warden Group's recovery contract was in place before it happened. Warden Group isn't here to cure Grayback County; it's here to recover Halvorsen's research and viable specimens for a client that was never named to the county, the state, or you. Marcus Voss's Fallow Company broke off when its own contractors figured this out first — and Voss may hold the one clean copy of Halvorsen's actual antidote research, not just the weaponized half.

09

Story Delivery & Endings

No cutscenes, no branching dialogue trees, no forced story missions. The mystery surfaces exactly the way B5's contract system and existing item-pickup architecture already work: found documents auto-file into a Notes/Intel tab on pickup (a light UI addition, not a new content pipeline), vendor barks drop hints opportunistically, and Dr. Anand's contract line is the one guaranteed thread that gates the endgame reveal behind actual play rather than a cutscene.

The resolution is a light branch, not a full narrative fork — three world-state flags, not three game endings with unique content:

  • Hand it to Warden Group — turn in the recovered cure research through Handler Okafor. Hub economy shifts (better Warden Group stock, Fallow Company escalates in retaliation).
  • Sell it to Reyes — the research goes wide on the black market. Warden Group's authority at the Lodge visibly weakens; new black-market stock unlocks.
  • Destroy it with Dr. Anand — no faction gets it. Quietest ending; a permanent world-state marker and closing vendor dialogue, no new content gated behind it.

This resolution is explicitly post-Stage-1 scope — it lives in Manifest 02's B5 phase, not anything blocking the core loop. Nothing here requires new engineering beyond flags Warden Group/Reyes/Anand vendor stock already needs to read.

10

Tone & Writing Rules

Guardrails for any future session (human or AI) writing contract text, vendor barks, or found documents against this bible.

  • Grounded survival dread, not camp. No zombie one-liners, no "brains" jokes. Infected are a biohazard, not a punchline.
  • The mystery is entirely optional content layered on freeform/contract play — never gate core survival mechanics behind it.
  • No forced dialogue trees or cutscenes. Every story beat is a found object, a bark, or a contract's flavor text.
  • Stay consistent with what's already mechanically true: two-tier infection (wound vs. bite), permadeath with loot-stays-where-it-fell, the hub/satellite split above. Don't write around them.
  • The exact contamination trigger stays unconfirmed on purpose — Tier 3 implies "field test," never states it as flatly proven. Certainty is a player inference, not a cutscene fact.
  • Warden Group NPCs (Okafor especially) are not cartoon villains — they believe the official story for a long time. The horror is institutional, not a mustache-twirling reveal.
